# Prompt template — Stage 6 · 360° rotation video per asset

**Model:** Seedance 2.0 (primary, orbit mode) | Veo (fallback) | Kling (if user requests)
**Duration:** 4-6 seconds
**Aspect:** 1:1 square (or 9:16 if the asset is tall — character full-body, weapons)
**Reference:** the still image of the asset (always attach as first-frame anchor)

## The hard rule

**Camera is LOCKED. Subject rotates.** This is the opposite of what most video models do by default — they want to dolly the camera, add cinematic motion, add lighting drift. All of that ruins a reference 360.

The output the user wants is identical to a turntable render in Blender / Marmoset: subject on the center, perfect full rotation, no other motion.

## Prompt template

```
Locked-camera turntable rotation of the subject in the reference image. The camera does NOT move — it is fixed on a tripod at eye-level distance. The subject rotates a smooth full 360 degrees around its own vertical axis, starting facing the camera, rotating clockwise back to facing the camera at the end of the clip. White background remains pure white throughout. Lighting stays neutral and flat throughout — no rim light appearing, no shadow shifting dramatically. No zoom, no dolly, no pan, no tilt. No camera shake. No motion blur on the subject. Sharp focus across the entire rotation. Reference-grade turntable, identical to a 3D software preview render.
```

## Per-asset overrides

### Full character 360
```
Locked-camera turntable of the full character from the reference image. Subject stays in A-pose throughout the rotation. Camera fixed at eye-level, framing the full body from head to toe in the center of the frame. Smooth clockwise 360 over 5 seconds. White background, neutral flat lighting throughout. No pose changes, no expression changes — pure rotation only.
```

### Accessory 360 (small object — ring, jewelry, prop)
```
Locked-camera turntable of the {ITEM_NAME} from the reference image. Object placed centered in the frame, rotating smoothly around its vertical axis a full 360 over 4 seconds. White background. Soft neutral lighting from above, no harsh shadow. No motion of the camera. Sharp macro focus held across the rotation.
```

### Long-asset 360 (weapon, staff, cape)
```
Locked-camera turntable of the {ITEM_NAME} from the reference image. Object held vertically in the center of the frame, rotating smoothly around its long axis a full 360 over 5 seconds. White background. Neutral flat lighting. No camera motion, no zoom.
```

## Universal negatives
```
no camera motion, no dolly, no zoom, no pan, no tilt, no shake, no cinematic lighting changes, no shadow drift, no rim light appearing mid-rotation, no environment, no background change, no pose change for the character, no expression change, no motion blur, no DoF breathing
```

## Why this is the hardest step

Video models are trained to make CINEMATIC video — moving camera, evolving light, dramatic shots. A reference turntable is the OPPOSITE of cinematic: it's a technical render where everything except the subject's rotation must hold completely still.

If Seedance/Veo refuses to honor "locked camera" after 2 attempts, try:
1. Strengthen the locked-camera language: "tripod-mounted security camera locked to one frame"
2. Add explicit anti-motion: "the only motion in the video is the subject rotating"
3. Reduce duration to 3 seconds (less time for the model to add drift)
4. Fall back to Veo with "orbit mode" if Seedance can't comply

## Cost / budget

Each 360 = 1 video render. For a full pack of 8 parts + 1 full character = 9 renders. Quote total cost to the user at Gate 2 BEFORE rendering. Offer "full character only" as the cheapest opt-out.

## Quality checklist per video

- [ ] Camera holds still — no drift in frame edges
- [ ] Background stays pure white — no shadow shift, no environment
- [ ] Lighting holds neutral — no rim light appearing, no key light changing color
- [ ] Subject completes a FULL 360 (ends facing camera again)
- [ ] No pose / expression change on a character 360
- [ ] No motion blur on the subject (the rotation should be smooth but each frame sharp)
- [ ] If 2 attempts fail: fall back to Veo, reduce duration, simplify prompt
